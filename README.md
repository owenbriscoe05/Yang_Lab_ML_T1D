# T1D Risk Prediction Project

Type 1 diabetes is a condition where the body destroys it's own pancreatic beta cells, leading to a complete loss of self-produced insulin. Insulin is the primary mechanism by which the body regulates its sugar concentration, and so T1D patients experience fatally high blood sugar without injecting exogenous (synthetic) insulin. 

Insulin resistance, sometimes termed "double diabetes", occurs when the body also becomes less responsive to insulin, requiring T1D patients to inject more and more to maintain stable glucose values. 

Insulin is a notorious up and down-regulator of various hormones in the body, and also causes significant fat retention when elevated in the body. It isn't yet clear to scientists why some patients develop significant insulin resistance and others don't. 

More broadly, some patients experience more trouble controlling their blood sugar levels, with only loose correlation to physiological factors like BMI and activity rate.

This project aims to eliminate some of that confusion by applying machine learning techniques to a cohort of T1D patients from the OneFlorida medical system, ultimately searching for hidden patterns and variables significantly affecting T1D resilience, defined here by comorbidity and mortality risk score.
